The method

Step 01

Decide the vibe

Night-time world map, deep ocean blue, land in sage green. A little gold butterfly does the flying, because obviously. Everything turns pink when it's flying to you. Every page starts as a few colour codes written at the very top, and the whole thing gets built from them.

/* the whole palette, decided before anything else */
--ocean: #0c2430;  --land: #2e5a4c;
--gold:  #ffc65a;  --pink: #ff8fb8;  /* yours */
Step 02

Draw the whole planet. By hand. Dot by dot.

The plan was to just download a world map. The internet said no, the download got blocked. So I drew every continent myself, as a list of longitude and latitude points going around each coastline. 678 of them. Here's the UK, which is basically just 20 dots holding hands:

// the UK, going clockwise from Cornwall
[[-5.7,50],[1.3,51.1],[1.7,52.6],[0.3,53.4],
 [-0.2,54.2],[-1.6,55.6], ... ]
// Middlesbrough lives around [-1.24, 54.57] btw
Step 03

Squash a round planet flat

Earth is a ball, screens are flat. To turn latitude and longitude into x and y on the screen, I used a map formula called the Miller projection. Longitude is easy (just spread it across the width). Latitude needs a bit of log-and-tangent maths so countries near the poles don't stretch into spaghetti.

const my = lat => -1.25 * Math.log(
  Math.tan(Math.PI / 4 + 0.4 * lat * Math.PI / 180)) * R;
const P = (lon, lat) => [(lon + 180) / 360 * W, my(lat) - Y0];
Step 04

Pick the animals and fact-check them

Twenty animals across the world, with extra butterflies and birds for you. For each one I wrote down where it lived, when it was last seen, and three facts, then double-checked them, because if I told you a wrong fact about a dodo you would never let it go.

{ n: "Dodo", ll: [57.55, -20.3], y: "Last seen c. 1662",
  f: ["It was basically a giant pigeon...", ...] }
Step 05

Teach a butterfly to flap

The wings are two shapes, a left one and a mirrored right one. Every frame, I squish them sideways using a sine wave, so they go wide, thin, wide, thin. That's literally it. Drag the slider to see what too much coffee looks like.

const flap = .3 + .7 * Math.abs(Math.sin(now / 95));
wings.setAttribute("transform", `scale(${flap},1)`);
Step 06

Make it actually fly somewhere

Between two stops, the butterfly follows a curve (a "bezier curve", fancy name for a rainbow-shaped path). While it flies, the camera zooms out so you can see where it's going, then zooms back in when it lands. The camera is just me changing which part of the map is visible, sixty times a second.

// position along the curve, t goes 0 → 1
x = (1-t)²·from + 2(1-t)t·lift + t²·to
// zoom out in the middle of the flight, back in at the end
cam.z = lerp(zoomStop, zoomMid, Math.sin(Math.PI * t));
Step 07

Draw twenty animals out of circles and blobs

No photos, no copying. Every animal is drawn in code from ellipses, rectangles and curvy paths. A tiger is an orange oval with five black squiggles on it. Then I gave them little animations: birds bob, butterflies flap, the dolphin swims, the big guys breathe.

Step 08

The last stop: you

I cropped your photo, blurred your work badge (privacy queen), shrank it, and turned the image into a long string of letters so it lives inside the code itself. Your name drops in letter by letter, the photo lands like a polaroid, and a swarm of butterflies and birds is drawn on a canvas, each one floating up on its own wobbly path.

// each butterfly in the swarm
b.y -= b.speed;
b.x += Math.sin(now / 900 + b.phase) * .9;  // the wobble
Step 09

Grow you a garden

Daffodils, tulips and lilies, all made in code. A daffodil is six ellipses spun around a centre with an orange dot in the middle. A lily is six pointy petals. Each flower starts squished to zero height and grows up out of the ground. Press the button, watch them sprout.

13 flowers, 3 kinds
Step 10

Put my voice in it

I recorded a voice note for every stop, 22 clips, almost 20 minutes total. Then I ran them through ffmpeg to even out the volume (so I'm not whispering at one stop and yelling at the next) and converted them to MP3. When the butterfly lands, my clip plays. When you tap next, it stops, so I never talk over myself.

ffmpeg -i New_Recording_59.m4a -ac 1 \
  -af loudnorm=I=-16 -b:a 64k 00.mp3   # x22
Step 11

Test it with a robot

I ran the page in a hidden robot browser that clicks Take off, waits, takes screenshots, and tells me if anything errored. It caught a few problems before you ever could.

Step 12

Pack it all into one file

The map, the drawings, your photo and all my voice notes got squeezed into a single HTML file, 12.8 MB. No internet needed, no extra files. Open it anywhere and it just works.
